The 30-demo programme

Thirty core demos across sixteen taught topics at roughly one per contact hour, plus ten backups. Twenty-six of the thirty end with each student submitting a number on Blackboard — designed as the engagement metric. Equation references are to your own lists: H/U/Q/D/N/W = 4A15 index, #n = 3A11 reference.

How the engagement metric works

  • Personalised parameters. Each submit-demo assigns the student a parameter from their student number (e.g. q = 0.20 + 0.03·d where d = last digit). The solver is deterministic: same parameter → same steady answer, so any submission can be spot-checked by re-running it. Copying a neighbour's number produces a mismatch with your own digit — the cheap fraud is self-marking.
  • Marking engagement, not accuracy. A generous tolerance band (say ±15% of the re-run value, or simply "a number was submitted in the window") scores the credit. The pooled plot the class builds is the pedagogical payoff, and outliers are discussion material, not lost marks.
  • Mechanics. One Blackboard test per hour, 1–2 numeric fields, open only during the slot. 26/30 core demos produce numbers (87% — comfortably past the 50% you need); the 4 exploratory demos can log a screenshot upload or nothing at all.
  • Standing rules (put them on every worksheet): everyone on the same Resolution: Medium; wait out the spin-up countdown; keep the tab visible (the sim pauses when hidden); after changing q, re-check any tailwater against yc = (q²/g)⅓ (printed on the q slider) and keep it ≥ 1.3 yc.
  • Prep flags. Demos marked DRY-RUN are sandbox builds or parameter choices I have not verified numerically — run them once before class. Scenes with verified numbers (hammer, h23, flumes, jet, venturi) carry no flag.

Three standard rigs (built once, reused)

Drawn geometry cannot yet be saved, so the reused rigs are recipes. Build each once in front of the class (60–90 s of drawing) or have students build from the card — the drawing is itself part of the engagement.

RIG-A · THE DUCT — Sandbox. A horizontal pressurised pipe: invert slab from x = 1.5 to 9 with top face at y = 2.0; soffit slab from x = 1.5 to 9 leaving a 0.40 m bore; reservoir wall above the pipe mouth at x = 1.5. Reservoir ON, head-driven, level per exercise. Used by FR-1, LL-1, LL-2, PU-1, B7, B10.
RIG-B · THE CHANNEL — Sandbox. A flat bed slab (top face y = 0.5) across the domain, reservoir feeding q on the left, tailwater or a drawn control on the right. Used by WE-1, FB-1, MO-1, and any weir/gate variant.
RIG-C · TWIN TANKS — Sandbox. Two open tanks with solid floors joined by a short pipe at the base; widths per exercise. Used by QS-2, DA-2, B9 (three tanks).

Dimensional analysis · 2–3 h · 3 demos

DA-1

The scale ladder

SUBMITDRY-RUN
Refs
D1, D13, D22–D23, D15 · Froude scaling and the π-collapse
Rig
RIG-B with a broad-crested weir block; class split into thirds building it at λ = 1, ½, ¼ (all lengths scaled, q scaled by λ1.5 from a personalised base q).
Submit
(λ, q, H over crest) — three numbers.
Payoff
Raw plot: three separate q–H curves. Replotted as Cd = q/(√g·H1.5) against H/P: one curve. The collapse is Buckingham. Keep the 2D caveat on a slide: per-metre q scales λ1.5, and the λ missing from the 3D λ2.5 is exactly the width the slice doesn't have (D23).
DA-2

Time scales as √λ

SUBMITDRY-RUN
Refs
D2, D23 (λt = √λ) · Q3 supplies the stopwatch

RIG-C, one tank with a base orifice, built at the student's assigned λ (tank width, head and orifice gap all scaled).
Submit
Time for the level to fall between two marked scaled depths (gauge trace).
Payoff
Pooled t vs λ on log axes: slope ½. The λ = ¼ builds drain in half the time of λ = 1 — kinematic similarity measured with a stopwatch.
DA-3

Scale effects, live

EXPLORATORY

Hydropower · 1–2 h · 2 demos

HP-1

Maximum power transmission: hf = H/3

SUBMITDRY-RUN
Refs
H2, H22–H26 · P = ρgQ(H − kQ²), peak where a third of the head is friction
Rig
?scene=hammer (49 m penstock under ~21 m head) with Cf raised to a prescribed value so friction matters; each student redraws the nozzle plate to their assigned gap (0.1–1.2 m).
Submit
(gap, q, jet velocity from the probe).
Payoff
Instructor computes P = ½ρqv² and hf = H − v²/2g per point; the pooled P–Q curve rises, peaks, falls — and at the peak, hf/H reads ≈ ⅓ (H24). Dry-run to choose the Cf that puts the optimum mid-range of the gap assignments.
HP-2

Why turbine buckets are cups, not plates

EXPLORATORY
Refs
H9–H14, #7 · F = ρQΔv on a vane; H11 jet velocity Cv√(2gH)
Run
Spout jet onto a drawn flat plate, then a curved cup that turns it back through ~165°, with a control-volume Force box reading the horizontal force on each: ≈4 kN/m against ≈7, close to the factor of two F = ρqv(1 − cos θ) predicts. Optionally the textbook deep-V, which reads only ≈5 because a wedge that deep cannot drain in the vertical plane. Velocity triangles and u/v₁ = ½ stay on slides — the sim supplies the physical intuition for ρQΔvw.
Submit
Nothing — a lecturer demonstration. The wheel is out of the tool’s reach (no rotating bodies), and so is the splitter: it divides the jet perpendicular to the wheel plane, which a vertical slice does not have. Measured rather than assumed — the record is in the exercise’s archive.

Natural channels & sediment transport · 3–4 h · 3 demos (reduced: no sediment motion)

NC-1

Slope-area method: estimate the mystery discharge

SUBMIT
Refs

Rig
?scene=m1 with the panel hidden or q unannounced (instructor sets a personalised q per student beforehand, or one shared mystery value).

NC-2

Is α really 1?

SUBMIT
Refs
N6, #44–46, #49–50 · energy and momentum coefficients
Rig
Any flowing channel scene; each student a personalised station x (and optionally free-slip toggled for contrast).
Run
Rake at your station. The chip prints umax, depth-average V and their ratio; read 4–5 profile points off the drawn curve (or APP.SIM.rake) and integrate α = Σu³Δy / (V³h) by mid-ordinate.
Submit
Your α (or, minimal version, the printed umax/V ratio).
Expect
α ≈ 1.05–1.2 in uniform reaches, spiking downstream of gates — the histogram is the day "assume α = 1" becomes a judged approximation (N6's compound-channel >2 case stays on slides: no lateral dimension).
NC-3

Bed shear and the riprap size

SUBMIT
Refs
N11–N13 · τ₀ = ρgRS, Shields 0.056, Dmin · #113
Rig
?scene=m2 (or s2 for a violent contrast), personalised q.
Run
Cursor prints h and Sf: τ₀ = ρg·h·Sf is a measurement. Then N13 on paper: Dmin = τ₀/[0.056(ρs − ρ)g] with ρs = 2650.

(τ₀, Dmin). Worked anchor: m2's defaults give τ₀ ≈ 50 N/m² → Dmin ≈ 55 mm — coarse gravel.

Pooled Dmin vs q on the steep scene spans sand → boulders. Honest framing on the worksheet: the stress side is simulated, the grains are not — this is the design calculation done on measured hydraulics.

Unsteady flow · 2–3 h · 3 demos

UN-1

The class discovers the celerity

SUBMIT
Refs
U11, U17–U18, U21, #53 · Joukowsky Δp = ρcΔu
Rig
?scene=hammer (validated: peak −5% of Joukowsky, period 3.0 s vs 4L/c = 2.8 s). Each student redraws the nozzle plate to a personalised gap → their own steady v₀.
Run
Probe the pipe velocity, drop a gauge mid-pipe, slam V, read the head jump off the square-wave trace.

UN-2

Flow establishment: the asymptotic start

SUBMIT
Refs
U1–U7 · inertia head; u_max = √(2gH/k); t = (l·u_max/2gH)·ln[(u_max+u)/(u_max−u)]
Rig
Establishment scene (estab): reservoir → 23 m pipe → valve, which boots CLOSED. Gauge mid-pipe, card set to plot speed.
Run
Open the valve; the speed trace rises asymptotically. Read umax and the time to reach 75% of it.
Submit
(umax, t75). Check: U6 at u = 0.75·umax gives t75 = ln 7 ·(l·umax/2gH) — a few seconds here, so no slow motion is needed.
Payoff
Personalised variant: each student a different reservoir level → pooled t75 against l·umax/2gH is a line of slope ln 7 (measured: 101% of it). The hammer scene cannot host this — there the rise finishes inside one wave transit.
UN-3

Surge tank: measure ymax against the ODE

SUBMITDRY-RUN
Refs
U23–U29 · the module's surge-tank ODE and trial-solved upsurge
Rig
Hammer scene + one drawn open standpipe (width per student, 0.5–2 m) teed into the pipe just upstream of the valve, tall enough to contain the surge.
Run
Establish flow, slam V, gauge the standpipe: the mass oscillation is the whole trace — upsurge, downsurge, friction decay.
Submit
(standpipe width bs, measured ymax, measured period).
Expect
Period near 2π√(l·bs/(g·bp)) — bs = 1 m on the 3 m bore gives ≈ 8 s. ymax sits below the frictionless bound v₀√(l·bp/(g·bs)); the gap IS the ku² term, and integrating U28/U29 to explain it quantitatively is the follow-up problem sheet.

WV-2

The buried wave gauge

SUBMIT
Refs
W5, W7 kernel · pressure attenuation cosh k(h+z)/cosh kh
Rig
?scene=wave (kh ≈ 0.7); half the class repeats in ?scene=wavedeep (kh ≈ 3.7).

WV-3

Reflection coefficient of a steep "sea wall"

SUBMIT
Refs
W21 · Krefl = Hr/Hi; sea walls 0.7–1.0, beaches 0.05–0.2
Rig
?scene=wavesurge — the 1:1.4 beach reflects strongly and builds a partial standing wave.
Run
Slide a gauge along the flume to find the largest (antinode) and smallest (node) depth oscillation amplitudes amax, amin.
Submit

What this is actually solving

Not a shallow-water model and not a smoke solver. This resolves the vertical plane, so velocity profiles, jets, rollers and pressure are in the picture rather than assumed away.

The equations are barotropic weakly-compressible Navier–Stokes on a staggered grid, with the cell fill fraction f doing double duty as the density:

∂f/∂t + ∇·(f u) = 0
∂u/∂t + (u·∇)u = −∇p/ρ + g + ∇·(ν∇u) − C_f|u|u/Δ
p/ρ = c² max(f − 1, 0)

The trick is that last line

It is a 2D Preissmann slot. Where a cell is not full, p = 0 and the water falls freely — that is the free surface, with no interface reconstruction needed. Where a cell is over-full the water is compressed and pressure travels at celerity c.

So free-surface flow and pressurised pipe flow are the same equations, the transition between them is automatic, and c is the slider that sets your water-hammer surge. There is no pressure Poisson solve — two GPU passes per substep, a few thousand substeps a second.

What is measured, not assumed

Normal depth and Manning’s n are read off the computed energy grade line, Sf = −dE/dx, not derived from the roughness slider. That matters because the effective resistance is not just C_f: the no-slip wall adds stress through the eddy viscosity, and a sloping bed rasterised onto a Cartesian grid is a staircase whose steps are genuine form roughness. At ~8 cells of depth the delivered n is ~0.07 almost regardless of the slider; at ~25 cells it falls towards 0.02. Depth relative to Δx is the lever, not the slider.

Honest limits

  • Celerity is not water’s. Real water hammer runs at ~1200 m/s; here c is 8–400 m/s because the time step scales with it. The scaling — ΔH = cΔv/g, period 4L/c — is exact; the absolute number is yours to choose.
  • Waves are damped by resolution. A wave must be tall enough in cells to survive an interface that is itself ~2 cells thick. No viscosity slider changes this; stroke and Δx do.
  • Plunging breakers are out of reach. The overturning tongue is thinner than the interface can hold, so breaks come out spilling.
  • Turbulence is a Smagorinsky closure at metre-ish resolution: good for the shape of a profile and the existence of a roller, not a boundary-layer calculation.

Scale

Gravity waves are Froude-scaled and there is no second length scale, so shrinking a flume geometrically buys nothing: Δx and the wave height shrink together and cells per wave height is unchanged. The levers are cell budget, domain shape, and steepness — which breaking caps at H/L ≈ 0.14.
